# Curriculum · African Heroes & Leaders

**Real people, told truthfully and warmly.** Age 4-8. Format: a short, factual mini-profile a
child can understand, framed by Zizi asking "who was that?" and Dede telling the story simply.

## Hard accuracy rule
Every hero is a **real, well-documented** figure. Dates, achievements, and country must be
**correct**; when a detail is uncertain, leave it out. Keep it **non-partisan** and dignified.
Prefer figures whose contribution is broadly celebrated. Note the source basis in the content
record. Never invent quotes or events.

## Format (per hero, one page + one future video)
- Who they were, where and roughly when (child-simple).
- The one big thing they are remembered for.
- One thing a child can take away ("she planted trees to heal the land", "he chose forgiveness").
- Zizi's question + Dede's answer framing.

## Seed set (well-documented, non-partisan)
- **Wangari Maathai** (Kenya) — planted millions of trees; first African woman to win the Nobel
  Peace Prize (2004). Takeaway: caring for the earth, one tree at a time. [SEED for this build]
- Nelson Mandela (South Africa) — chose reconciliation after prison; first democratically elected
  president. Takeaway: forgiveness and fairness.
- Queen Amina (Zazzau, historical Hausa land) — a warrior queen and builder.
- Kwame Nkrumah (Ghana) — led Ghana to independence; pan-African unity.

Add more only when facts are verified. Keep the tone celebratory, simple, and true.
