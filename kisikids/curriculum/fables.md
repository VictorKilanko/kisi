# Curriculum · Fables & Folktales

**Traditional African folktales, retold simply, with their origin noted.** Age 3-7. Format: an
illustrated read-along "book" (page by page), framed by Dede + Zizi, ending on the gentle lesson.

## Rules
- State the **origin/tradition** of each tale (e.g. Yoruba, Akan/Ashanti, pan-African) in the
  content record. These are traditional stories retold, not invented; do not claim authorship.
- Keep the lesson gentle and kind. Trim anything frightening. Short sentences, one idea per page.

## Format (per fable, one read-along + one future video)
- 4-8 short "pages", each an illustration + 1-2 sentences.
- A tiny frame: Zizi asks for a story, Dede tells it, Zizi names the lesson at the end.

## Seed set
- **Why the Tortoise Has a Cracked Shell** (a widely-told West African / Yoruba "Ìjàpá" tortoise
  tale; also known across the continent) — greed and the birds' feast; the tortoise falls; the
  shell is mended in patches. Lesson: share, and listen to friends. [SEED for this build]
- Anansi and the Pot of Wisdom (Akan/Ashanti, Ghana) — wisdom is for sharing.
- The Lion and the Mouse-style kindness tales (note the specific tradition when chosen).

Cite the tradition for each in the content record; keep retellings faithful in spirit.
