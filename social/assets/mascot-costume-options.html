Mascot Costume — Design Options

Four full-costume directions for the Kisi mascot, a hen who greets every visitor first. All four are built from the site’s brand palette and keep the character’s signature red comb, warm plumage, and bright welcoming eyes. The name is still being decided, so the seal, sash medallion, and chest patch are left blank on purpose — drop the final name and national seal in once chosen. These are wearable-costume concepts (a person inside the suit), drawn front-on so a fabricator can pattern from them.

Option 1

The Welcomer

“No office, all heart.” The everyday face.

Best for: daily meet-and-greets, farm visits, school groups, most photos.

Head
Oversized hen head, three-bump red comb, big friendly eyes, soft short beak.
Body
Terracotta plush plumage, cream belly panel, darker wing sweeps at the sides.
Signature
Green presidential sash with gold trim + a round seal medallion (blank for now).
Feet
Natural three-toe hen feet in gold-orange; performer wears matched shoe covers.
Fabricator notes: foam-and-fleece head on a helmet cage, mesh vision panel behind the comb. Sash is a removable Velcro band so the medallion art can be reprinted when the name/seal is final. Keep the belly panel cream for contrast in photos.
Option 2

Sunday Best

Agbada & fila. Dignity with a wink.

Best for: launches, festivals, cultural days, VIP and press moments.

Head
Same hen head, comb peeking under an indigo fila cap with gold band.
Body
Flowing green agbada-style robe with wide draped sleeves.
Signature
Gold geometric neck embroidery inspired (respectfully) by southwestern Nigerian dress.
Feet
Hen feet show below the hem; optional woven raffia sandals for events.
Fabricator notes: robe is a separate over-layer so the base suit (Option 1) can be reused underneath. Use lightweight breathable fabric — the performer will be warm. Keep embroidery an original geometric motif; do not copy a specific real family or brand pattern.
Option 3

Farm Ambassador

Apron on, boots ready. Down to earth.

Best for: markets, egg-day stalls, kids’ activities, hands-on farm content.

Head
Standard hen head, full comb, bright eyes — the most approachable read.
Body
Terracotta plumage with an indigo work apron and a blank gold chest patch.
Signature
Gold neckerchief + yellow farm wellington boots (performer’s real footwear).
Feet
Practical rubber boots — safest and easiest for the performer to move in.
Fabricator notes: the most economical build and the most durable for outdoor use. Apron and neckerchief are removable, so this shares the same base suit as Option 1. Chest patch is a Velcro badge — swap in the final name/logo later.
Option 4

Festival Star

Full plumage, full drama. The showstopper.

Best for: parades, big reveals, stage appearances, hero shots and merch art.

Head
Cream-feathered head, full comb, and a small gold star crown above it.
Body
Bright cream body with gold outline for stage lighting; green sash + gold seal.
Signature
Big fanned tail of alternating gold and green feathers — the wow factor.
Feet
Hen feet with gold anklets; add a small internal frame to support the tail fan.
Fabricator notes: the tail needs a light backpack frame so it holds its shape and doesn’t catch wind — test outdoors. Heaviest and hottest build; reserve for short appearances. This is also the strongest silhouette for logo/merch illustration.

How these work together

  • One base suit, three looks. Options 1, 2 and 3 share the same hen head and body; only the removable layer (sash, agbada, apron) changes. Build the base once, then the sash/robe/apron as add-ons.
  • Option 4 is the special-events build. Bigger, hotter, and best for short, high-impact appearances and for merch/illustration art.
  • Name is intentionally blank. The seal medallion, chest patch and fila band are placeholders. Once the mascot name and national seal are final, print them onto Velcro badges and swap them in.
  • Welfare of the performer: mesh vision panel, internal cooling/fan, water breaks, and a handler at all outdoor events. Nigerian heat is the real design constraint — keep fabrics light and breathable.
